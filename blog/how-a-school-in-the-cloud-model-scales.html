How a “School in the Cloud” model scales without losing its soul

20 August 2025

Learning DesignVision & Future of Education

Glocal: Global Network, Local Impact

When people first hear global education network, they imagine the same template rolled out everywhere — a franchise of identical classrooms.

That’s not what we’re building.

Our model starts with a simple principle: education works best when it is locally rooted — but globally connected.

Why scale?

The challenges facing learners are increasingly global:

  • Climate change.
  • AI and automation.
  • Migration and cultural shifts.

But the ways we respond must be culturally specific, shaped by local history, language, and needs. Scaling isn’t about cloning one model. It’s about sharing a common backbone while allowing each community to design its own heart.

The Cloud as the Connector

The “School in the Cloud” is not a single school — it’s an ecosystem. Our cloud infrastructure lets schools:

  • Share resources instantly.
  • Collaborate across time zones.
  • Co-create curriculum projects that bring multiple cultures into the same learning experience.

This is how a learner in Nairobi can build a Storymap about coastal erosion with a partner in Cornwall — and both can bring their own perspective to the same challenge.

Local Ownership, Global Standards

Our framework ensures:

  • Shared values — learner agency, co-creation, inclusive practice.
  • Shared tools — a global LMS spine, adaptable rubrics, common governance protocols.
  • Local autonomy — communities decide what to teach, in what language, and through which cultural lens.

How We Scale Without Losing Depth

  1. Start with a Lighthouse We establish one “lighthouse” school in each region — a fully functioning hybrid model that trains educators locally.
  2. Build the Local Mesh Surrounding schools join through the Novus Learning Network, sharing resources and mentorship.
  3. Grow International Links As the network matures, thematic projects connect regions: art and science exhibitions, climate action campaigns, multilingual storytelling festivals.
  4. Keep Iterating Every new school adapts the framework, then feeds its insights back into the network. It’s a living system, not a frozen manual.

The Impact

  • Learners develop global citizenship alongside deep local knowledge.
  • Teachers access a peer network far beyond their immediate geography.
  • Schools can innovate faster, drawing on tested ideas from anywhere in the network.

Why This Matters Now

The next generation will inherit problems that no single region can solve alone. Building a mesh of schools that share a vision — but adapt to their local context — is how we equip them for that reality.
