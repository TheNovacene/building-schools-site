Portable Learner Records: A Single Central Register That Actually

1 October 2025

Learning DesignTechnology & ToolsVision & Future of Education

Every time a child moves school, their story risks being lost. A new teacher, a new SENCo, a new social worker—and suddenly the paperwork resets. Parents repeat themselves. Assessments are re-done. Hours are wasted, and children fall through the cracks.

We already solved this in health. Your NHS number (in theory at least) makes your medical history accessible wherever you go. Education has no such spine.

The Problem With Current Systems

Most schools and alternative provisions use siloed databases: MIS, TutorCruncher, Google Classroom, spreadsheets. Each one works for its local purpose—but none of them travel with the learner. The result:

  • EHCPs don’t transfer cleanly.
  • Safeguarding notes are inconsistently passed on.
  • Attendance and progress data are trapped in PDFs.
  • Parents shoulder the burden of being the “single source of truth.”

A Simple, Radical Fix

Imagine a portable learner record: a secure central register, with fields for education, health, safeguarding, and support needs. Role-based access means a SENCo sees different data than a tutor, and a social worker sees different data again. Families can add their own notes.

When a child moves provision, the record moves with them. No duplication. No re-entry. No lost history.

Why Now?

The Children’s Commissioner has already called for a single identifying number for every child. The government is moving toward it. But waiting for a top-down system could take years. Schools and local authorities can start prototyping this now, using existing secure platforms.

Quick Wins

  • Build your “single central register” on a scalable database (IBM, for example).
  • Standardise EHCP, medical, and safeguarding fields.
  • Pilot portability between two institutions (e.g. a mainstream school and an AP).
  • Give families access to their child’s live profile.

What This Changes

  • No more repeating trauma at every new school gate
  • Faster handovers between settings.
  • Less duplication of assessments.
  • A joined-up view of the whole child, not just fragments.

Portable learner records aren’t about bureaucracy. They’re about dignity, continuity, and trust. If health can do it, education can too.
