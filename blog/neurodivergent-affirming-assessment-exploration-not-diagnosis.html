Neurodivergent-Affirming Assessment: Exploration, Not Diagnosis

6 October 2025

Learning DesignTechnology & Tools

Most school assessments feel like judgement. Scores, rankings, gaps to fill. For neurodivergent learners, it often feels worse: another hoop, another label, another reminder of not fitting the mould.

At Haven, we’re trying a different approach. Assessment as exploration.

The Problem With Traditional Assessment

  • It measures conformity, not capacity.
  • It frames difference as deficit.
  • It pressures learners into performance before trust is built.
  • It often doubles as a gatekeeping tool for support, forcing families into diagnosis-hunting.

Exploration Instead of Judgement

What if assessments weren’t about “what’s wrong” but “how do you think, communicate, and grow best”?

That means:

  • Opt-in, low-pressure screenings. Learners choose to engage when they feel safe.
  • Creative evidence. Projects, reflections, and tasks that reveal thinking styles.
  • Strength-first reports. Highlighting pattern recognition, creativity, persistence—not just gaps.
  • Co-design. Involving learners in shaping how they want to be assessed.

The Toolkit

Free/low-cost tools already exist. Paired with relational practice, they become powerful:

  • Dyslexia South screeners (literacy + maths).
  • Learner-designed projects to showcase skills in context.
  • Reflective prompts instead of timed tests.

Why It Matters

Assessment should give us insight, not injury. It should help us understand learners in all their complexity, without reducing them to scores.

For neurodivergent children especially, assessment can either reinforce trauma—or open a door to self-knowledge and support.

Exploration is how we choose the latter.
