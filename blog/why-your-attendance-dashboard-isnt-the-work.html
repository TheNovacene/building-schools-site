Why Your Attendance Dashboard Isn’t the Work (and What Is)

24 September 2025

Systems & StrategyTechnology & Tools

Every new school or alternative provision hits the same moment: “Can you build us an attendance dashboard?”

Of course you can. The APIs exist. The graphs look neat. Everyone feels reassured. But here’s the truth: an attendance dashboard doesn’t fix attendance.

Dashboards vs. Decision Loops

A dashboard is a mirror. It tells you what already happened. A decision loop is a lever. It changes what happens next.

If a child is missing school, the work isn’t a prettier chart. The work is:

  • Who notices first?
  • Who makes the call home?
  • What language do they use?
  • What support is offered, and when?
  • Where is it logged, and how is it followed up?

Without these loops, the dashboard is just a decorative mirror reflecting back the same problem.

Founder Danger Zone

As a founder or consultant, this is where scope creep lurks. Build one dashboard, and you’ll be asked for five more: engagement, behaviour, safeguarding, wellbeing. You can spend months wiring up data feeds while the core issue—relationship and response—remains untouched.

What To Build Instead

  • Clear triggers: e.g. “Two missed sessions = mentor call within 24 hours.”
  • Shared language: scripts that balance accountability with care.
  • Visible escalation map: so staff and families know exactly what happens next.
  • Feedback loops: parents and learners can flag if the system feels supportive or punitive.

The Dashboard Test

A good dashboard should only answer one question: where should we act next? If it can’t direct a human response, it isn’t serving the child.

Dashboards don’t change attendance. People do. And the real work is building the loops that let them act with speed, coherence, and compassion.
