One of the most important foundations in this journey is the creation of a systems account—an invisible backbone to the digital learning environments we’re building. While it may not be the most glamorous topic, it’s one of the most critical. Without it, online schools can’t hope to scale securely or efficiently.

What is a Systems Account, and Why Does It Matter?

A systems account serves as the digital morality of your online school. It’s not a personal account tied to an individual but rather a master account that controls the architecture of your organisation’s digital tools. It creates the framework for how everything from shared drives to user permissions operates, ensuring security, scalability, and, most importantly, protection for our children’s data and wellbeing.

By setting up a systems account, online schools can create a foundation that:

  • Ensures Security: Creates a robust foundation that keeps sensitive data and core controls hidden from prying eyes, enabling effective safeguarding.

  • Interrogates Values: Encourages architects to deeply reflect on what they are trying to achieve—not just in functionality, but in the ethos and culture their digital space will cultivate.

  • Supports Scalability: Provides the structure to grow your school while ensuring the digital space adapts to the needs of both educators and students without falling into chaos.

  • Promotes Safeguarding: Helps manage permissions and monitor activity to protect students and staff, ensuring a safe and trusted learning environment.

Practical Steps to Create a Systems Account

Here’s how to set up a systems account and lay the groundwork for a secure and efficient online school:

  1. Create a Systems Account: Set up an email address like systems@youronlineschool.com. Assign Super Admin permissions to this account. Restrict its use to architecture management and audits—never for day-to-day operations.

  2. Secure the Account: Use a strong password and enable two-factor authentication (2FA). Configure email settings to block all external communication to and from this account.

  3. Establish a Shared Framework: Design Shared Drives for key areas like staff resources, student resources, and administrative files. Use the systems account to manage permissions and access controls for these drives.

  4. Set Up Organisational Units (OUs): Group users into OUs based on roles (e.g., staff, students). Apply tailored policies for each group (e.g., restrict external email for students).

  5. Create Role-Specific Admin Accounts: Establish an operational admin account (e.g., admin@youronlineschool.com) for day-to-day tasks like managing Google Classroom and Calendar.

  6. Audit and Monitor: Use tools like Google Vault to set retention policies for emails and files. Regularly review activity logs to ensure compliance and spot any anomalies.
