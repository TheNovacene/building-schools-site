What a Speed-Awareness Course Taught Me About Online Pedagogy

13 October 2025

Educator DevelopmentLearning Design

Last month, I had to take an online speed-awareness course. I expected boredom and box-ticking. What I got instead was a masterclass in online learning design.

It made me rethink how we teach, especially when the learners are at home, distracted, or reluctant to engage.

Strict, but clear

The rules were non-negotiable:

  • Camera on.
  • Mic on.
  • Eyes on screen — the facilitator even noticed if you were glancing away.

At first it felt intrusive. But very quickly, it produced something rare: absolute attention.

Perfectly sequenced instructions

The tutor had clearly thought about cognitive load.

  • No clutter on slides.
  • Short, precise directions.
  • Step-by-step tasks: “Write this down, hold it up, show me.”

Each moment was predictable, and because of that, safe.

Unexpected engagement

By the end, I realised:

  • I’d stayed fully present.
  • I’d actually learned something.
  • And I wasn’t resentful — because the structure freed me to focus.

The lesson for schools

In online classrooms we often over-compensate:

Too soft → endless tolerance for cameras-off, mics-muted, half-listening.

Too harsh → blanket rules that trigger pushback, especially for neurodivergent learners.

This course showed me the middle way: clear expectations, tight design, consistent follow-through.

Not every learner can tolerate “camera on, mic on” — and we must respect that. But what we can borrow is the rigour of design:

  • Minimalist slides.
  • Micro-instructions.
  • Active participation every few minutes.
  • Clear feedback loops.

Why this matters

If even a reluctant adult (me!) can finish a mandatory course thinking, “That was actually effective,” imagine what could happen if we designed all online lessons with that level of clarity and sequencing.

✨ I’m curious: have you ever sat in on a training or compliance course and thought, “This is better designed than half the school lessons I’ve seen”? What did you take away?
